# 0.7.107: The next line enters over the edge

**Upcoming text no longer waits on screen.** With vertical Scroll and Wait for line on, blank rows between two text lines are now a pause with a separate exit and entry. The finished line, and every row before it, leaves the moment it ends on one run at the usual rate. The next line, and every row after it, waits out of sight just beyond the new-line edge, with all of its paint outside the window. It then enters over the edge on its own run at the same rate and settles 0.1 s before its first word. 0.7.106 parked the next line in the slot below the current one for the whole wait.

**The reported cue.** Four visible lines, current line 2, 0.18 s at 0.7x (one ordinary scroll takes 0.257143 s), Pause Line Length 0.1, with and without the saved line position styles. "All right, that's $160" ends at 15.226667 s, followed by two authored blank rows, then "and we'll get this" at 16.875 s. It now leaves at 15.226667 s on a three-row run and is out of the window by 15.826667 s. The window then stays empty and still. "and we'll get this" enters on a three-row run from 16.175 s, settles at 16.775 s and highlights its first word at 16.875 s. Scrolling down, there is one slot above the current one, so it waits two rows out and enters on a two-row run, settling at the same time.

**Short gaps.** Some gaps are shorter than the run the finished line makes. In those, the entry still comes from the edge on a full run at the usual rate, and it starts early enough to settle 0.1 s before the first word. So it can start while the line before is still being spoken. The next line is then already moving as it comes into view, never shown waiting in place. It never comes closer to the rows before it than their settled spacing, so it settles no sooner than they do. It also doesn't set off before the previous run has settled. When the finished line's own run outlasts the gap, the next line settles together with it, inside the 0.1 s lead or, in the tightest gaps, just after its first word. It is never early or parked, and the word still highlights on its own time.

- "…booked a pickup." ends at 13.106667 s, with one blank row before "All right" at 13.586667 s. 0.7.106 showed "All right" waiting in Line 4 from 12.34 s. It now enters from three rows out at 12.935238 s scrolling up, or two rows out at 13.106667 s scrolling down. It settles with "…pickup." at 13.535238 s, 0.051 s before its first word highlights.
- "putting it off." ends at 8.548333 s, with one blank row before "Got a quote from." at 8.975 s. "Got a quote from." enters from 8.377 s scrolling up, or 8.548 s scrolling down. It settles with "putting it off." at 8.977 s, 0.002 s after its first word, as in 0.7.106.

**Placed by its own slot.** An entering line is placed by the slot it is heading for, with its own size, line position style and paint outsets. Rows leaving before it never move it. It waits far enough out that all of its paint is beyond the new-line edge: outline, shadow, background and negative margins included. Its paint first reaches the window at that edge and then comes in at the shared rate, at most one frame's travel at a time. This holds when Current, Next and Past have different sizes. In an earlier 0.7.107 candidate, with Current at 60 px, Next and Past at 30 px and four 60 px slots, "After." was stacked under the 60 px line before it and the 30 px blank. Its whole glyph (top 210, bottom 240) was already inside the window the first time it was drawn, so it popped in. It now crosses the edge, scrolling either way and with the current line two or three slots from that edge. It still does so with Next larger than Current, with an outline, shadow, background and negative margins, with line height under 1, and with line position styles of other sizes and margins. When a word changes state as it arrives, for example a first word growing to its 60 px Current size as it highlights, that word's own size and paint change at that moment. That is an authored style change, not part of the entry. Explicit boundary settings still control clipping.

**Same rate, same styling.** Exit and entry each ease in, cruise at 1.5 rows per Transition duration ÷ Speed and ease out, with the same profile as any other run. Each row keeps its usual line position style and word styling, and exit and entry meet in the same settled layout an ordinary scroll would reach. With line position styles, slot margins blend as the entering line passes, so it moves in small steps and can be nudged back briefly, as in an ordinary scroll.

**Unchanged.** Lines with no blank between them still hold until the scroll is due, or leave when the line before ends. The opening run has no line to leave, so it still waits and settles the first line 0.1 s before it is spoken. Trailing blanks still go as soon as the last line ends. Fade, Slide and Pop, Wait for line off and horizontal pages are unchanged. Transition duration 0 steps at once: the finished line leaves when it ends and the next line appears in place 0.1 s before its first word.

**Tests.** Core unit tests run the whole saved document in both scroll directions (its words are in `native/tests/saved_document_words.inc`). All nine pauses are covered: two of one blank row, three of two, one each of four and nine, and two of five, four of them shorter than the finished line's run. At every pause, nothing still to be spoken is drawn in view before its entry, it enters over the edge, and glyphs of different rows never overlap. The reported cue, the "…pickup." cue and the "Got a quote from." cue are also checked separately, with round and as-stored times and with and without line position styles. A sweep of blank counts, speeds and current-line positions, a brief line before a long pause and Transition duration 0 are covered too. A CoreText raster test checks the unequal-size entry in real pixels: no Next ink before the entry, first ink at the new-line edge, and at most a frame's travel per frame, with and without decoration and after short and long pauses. The compiled adapter smoke test now compares Pause Line Length at 4 s with the current line at the bottom. There, Auto takes "Third." out of the window while 2 s still holds it in the top slot.

**Not yet verified in Premiere.** This is proven by unit, raster and compiled-adapter tests only, not yet by a check in the running app.
