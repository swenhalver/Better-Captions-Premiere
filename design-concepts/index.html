One workspace. Two focused tabs.

The transcript and word timeline work together. Styling gives past, current, and next words their own appearance.

Interactive mockup · Sample data · No Premiere connection.

Read the sentence. Shape its timing.

Choose a sentence in the transcript to open its words in one timeline lane. Your sentence stays in view while you work on a word.

  • Select a word in the phrase or timeline.
  • Drag either edge, enter seconds, or nudge one frame. Connected edges move together.
  • No overlaps. Boundaries stop before a word loses its last frame. Gaps between words remain usable space.

Style every stage of the sentence.

Past words, the current word, and next words have independent settings. Switch between them without losing the selected sentence.

  • Typography: font, weight, size, fill, opacity, spacing, case and italic.
  • Stroke and shadow: separate colors, widths, opacity, blur and offsets.
  • Word bubbles: fill, opacity, padding and corners.
  • Caption layout and animation: shared controls in the same Styling tab.

Place the text. Keyframe its area.

In Styling, Position & size controls the selected caption’s center, width, and height. Each property has a stopwatch and previous, add/remove, and next keyframe buttons.

  • Scrub the sample playhead to inspect interpolated values.
  • Set Linear, Ease in/out, or Hold for the segment after a keyframe.
  • Keep type size fixed. Width controls wrapping; height remains independent.

This is the proposed combined interface. Per-state stroke and shadow extend the existing controls; they still need renderer integration. Text-area keyframes work on sample values here. Connecting these panel controls to native Motion still needs integration. The type sample demonstrates appearance; on-video placement and animation playback require Premiere.
