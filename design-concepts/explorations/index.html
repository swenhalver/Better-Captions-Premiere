Three ways to work with captions.

The same Premiere-native tool, organized around three different editing habits. Click words, adjust timing, and try the appearance controls.

Interactive conceptsSample transcript. No Premiere connection.

Transcript desk

Read, correct, and time words in one continuous workspace.

Best for everyday editing.

The transcript stays visible while a word inspector follows your selection. Appearance is one click away.

Tradeoff: precise timing is less visual.

Timing desk

See the rhythm of a phrase. Give each word its exact moment.

Best for detailed timing.

Words become selectable time ranges. Start, end, duration, and a sample playhead stay together.

Tradeoff: fewer captions are visible at once.

Style inspector

Build the look around upcoming, current, and spoken words.

Best for visual finishing.

Appearance controls lead. A compact caption selector keeps text and timing within reach.

Tradeoff: transcript correction takes another step.

My starting point: Transcript desk.

Keep the transcript as the main workspace, bring the Timing desk’s word ranges into its inspector, and use the Style inspector for appearance. That gives frequent tasks the fewest clicks while keeping the narrow panel calm.

The frame nudges and “Set to playhead” buttons are proposed shortcuts. Direct word selection inside Premiere’s Program Monitor is outside these concepts; words are selected in the panel.
